The Historic Shift: From Word Counting to Link Democracy

Before Google launched in 1998, early search engines like AltaVista, Lycos, and Yahoo! ranked web pages primarily by keyword frequency (counting how many times the searched word appeared in the text).

⚠️ Old Way (1997): Keyword Counting

  • The Logic: If a page says "pizza" 10 times, it must be 10x more relevant than a page that says it once.
  • The Fatal Flaw: Spammers quickly realized they could write "pizza pizza pizza..." in tiny white text on a white background (keyword stuffing).
  • The Result: The spam site pizza-pizza-pizza.com easily stole Rank #1, pushing authentic restaurants and recipes out of sight!

🌟 Surfer Way (1998): PageRank Simulation

  • The Logic: Hyperlinks represent human recommendations. If many reputable websites link to you, your page is valuable.
  • The Random Surfer Model: Larry Page imagined an internet user who clicks random links 85% of the time, and gets bored and jumps anywhere 15% of the time.
  • The Victory: Because genuine sites link to best-pizza-pune.com (4 in-links) and nobody in the community links to the spammer (0 in-links), PageRank pushes the spammer straight to the bottom!
💡 Takeaway for Students: A webpage can mention a keyword 50 times and still have almost 0% PageRank. Conversely, an authoritative page mentioning a word just once or twice can rank #1 if trusted hubs point to it.
